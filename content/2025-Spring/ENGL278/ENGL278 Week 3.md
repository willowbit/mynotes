After discussing in class what I missed in the reading, I'm able to better understand Wilson's meaning by "A Journey to the End of Taste." Like him, when thinking about popular media that seems to evade me, I often think, "well, if thousands of people find something of worth in this, than there must be something I'm missing." For me this turns into a kind of FOMO: look how much love and excitement these people get out of this, I want to experience that joy too!

This has resulted in what I call getting into musical artists "manually." Rather than cherrypicking what I like from an artist, I hammer my brain with three or four of their most highly regarded albums until it all clicks. It usually takes a few months, but it always works.

Drew Daniel's discussion of the Proust quote uncovers a vital truth that we should respect "even the artwork we dislike" out of a recognized ignorance of it's historical and cultural context (226). This is supported by Wilson's conversations with Céline Dion's fans who each had a historical and cultural connection with Dion that Wilson didn't, whether that be through her Canadian background or adoption as a gay icon.

That being said, although Wilson lacked the connection that drew many of Dion's superfans to her music, through his conversations with others he was able to find a deeper appreciation for music he had despised beforehand. For me, Wilson's "End of Taste" is more about a wider accessibility of music than is currently perceived. My hobby of "manually" getting into artists hasn't failed yet, and has proven that, with some determination, I can learn to enjoy 100 year old folk just as easily as I can 2010 experimental gay furry rock. And if that's true, what is "taste" anyway? Is it just a box we've built around ourselves to protect our cultural pride?
