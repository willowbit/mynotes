## 4.9

Witch: a person who practices witchcraft (magic)
- Old English: wicce (f): witch, sorceress & wicca (m): witch, sorcerer, warlock
- key: magic obtained through pacts with the Devil (uses power to destroy) → "orgies" of the witches' sabbath (cf. Wild Hunt)
- represent: fear of anarchy & disorder → esp. tied to women
- changing imagination: from an individual witch in antiquity (e.g. Circe) → collective, diabolical army out of corrupt humanity

**Witch Hunting in Europe**

Height of European Witch Hunting: territories of the Holy Roman Empire from 1580 to 1630 → especially Germany
- reality: major witch hunts were sporadic & rarer than sources imply
- total: c. 80,000 victims in Europe and its colonies (e.g. Salem)
- victims: anyone could be accused → tortured (mental and physical); high numbers of confession because only way to end torture → hang or burn
- made famous: print culture, rise of pamphlets and newspapers

Why Women? c. 75% of accused and higher percentage of executions
- Devil is male → witches = submissive (female) via sexual congress
- Aristotelian bias: women = defective/imperfect men → inferior, therefore more susceptible to corruption
- legally vulnerable (no legal status), need male defense → widows and spinsters
- witchcraft centered on attacking fertility/health → female domains of reproduction, childbirth, nurturing
