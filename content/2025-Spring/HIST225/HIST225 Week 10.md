## 4.14

Postcolonialism: historical period/perspective representing the aftermath of Western colonialism (imperialism)
- focus: centering the history & agency of subjugated peoples

Creole Vernacular Religions: how traditions persist within colonialism
- examples: Yoruba (Nigeria), Santeria (Cuba), Vodun (Haiti) → African ancestral traditions brought ot the Americas via involuntary migration (New World Slavery)
- creolization as "ritual piracy": combine religious practices for survival → mix Christianity of colonizers with ancestral traditions (e.g., an altar with a cross & other deities/objects)
- resistance to colonizers: hiding ancestral traditions in plain sight → the gods = Catholic saints

Creole Magic: a form of communication between humans & spirits (dead) shaped by historical conditions (esp. enslavement)
- how: summon unseen world via spirit-imbued objects
- creativity: tap into symbolic power & gestures of Christian ritual

Black Conjure Culture: magical tradition of invoking spiritual power
- practical magic: healing, wealth, romance, protection/self-defense
- creolization with Christianity: attending to physical & spiritual well-bring not being met in diasporic context

Conjurers: the practitioners of conjure
- 17th c. "cunning men/witches" → 19th c. "root workers" (healers) vs. "conjure doctors" (harm and heal) & "trick/witch doctors" (harm)
- extraordinary powers (weather, invisible, fly, shapeshift, spiritual contact) but so outsiders / marginalized figures
- unusual physical features & habits: birthmarks, harelips, red eyes or eyes of different colors; tinted glasses (second sight), staffs/canes, crossdressing; strange births (7th chilid of a 7th child)

A conceptual framework of black agency/power: inaccessible to outsiders (enslavers, etc.)
- defense against trauma of slavery (psychological, physical)
- small, disabling acts as resistance (vandalism, illicit doctoring, theft)

## 4.16

Tarot (French from Italian tarocco [?] "foolish"): form of divination using a card deck to interpret past, present, future
- goal: self-discovery of querent ("seeker") → guidance, insight
- meanings: based upon position in spread in response to question

78 Cards in Deck: two arcanas...
- arcana: Latin arcanus/a/um: "hidden, private, mysterious" → specialized knowledge that is mysterious to the uninitiated
- marjor arcana: 22 cards representing major life themes
- minor arcana: 56 cards divided into 4 suits (wands, pentacles, swords, cups)

The Origins of Tarot: from elaborate playing cards → divination tool within the occult revivals in the 18th and 19th centuries
- inventor: Duke Filippo Maria Visconti of Milan (15th c.) → commissioned cards to entertain aristocrats at parties
- mass production: Italian Wars (16th c.) spread game → standardized "Tarot de Marseille" (78 cards in four suits, 21 major arcana + fool)
- mystic connections (18th c.): Antoine de Gebelin (pastor  occultist) → cards concealed ancient Egyption book of Thoth

Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn (19th c. England)
- middle-class secret society: based on Freemasons & dedicated to Renaissance forms of mysticism (alchemy, kabbalah, astrology, tarot, geomancy) from prints of cipher manuscripts & archaelogical discoveries (especially Egypt)
- tarot: tied each card to 22 pathways between Kabbalistic sephiroth on the Tree of Life = divinatory interpretation to each

Kabbalah ("to receive"; est. 12th-13th c. Spain): Jewish mystical tradiition on hidden knowledge of God
- Tree of Life: symbolic diagram of the divine structure of creation and interconnectedness of all existence via sephiroth (spheres) and 22 pathways (lines)
- Sephiroth (circles): 10 attributes of God = energies that balance each other
- Pathways (lines): 22 letters of the Hebrew alphabet
- so what: visual representation of the path of return to God

## 4.18

A Familiar: an attendant spirit companion of witches, often in animal form
- purpose: displays relationship between witch and devil via contact with familiar → the "material embodiment of a demonic pact"
- represent: concerns over rise in pet-keeping amongst non-elite classes (not just beasts of labor) & anxiety over the permeable boundary between the human and non-human
- so what: led to perseuction of people with personal pets

Types of Familiars in (English) Witch Hunts: ubiquitous and common
- personal pets (dogs, cats)
- small outdoor animals near or in house (toads, rabbits, mice, insects)
- any animals with unusual appearances

Fae: a race of super or sub-human reatures that interact with humans at the thin places (where worlds connect, e.g. fairy rings)
- represent: bridges/connectors to natural world by societies feeling alienated by civilization
- the uncanny valley: fae share many common human characteristics or habits but do so in slightly off ways
- historical anxieties about abduction & loss: steal people and replace with changelings (false dupes), humans crossing over and becoming slaves
- present interests (New Age): re-enchanting a disenchanted world → an overly romanticized view
