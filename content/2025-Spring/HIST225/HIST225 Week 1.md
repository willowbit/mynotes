## 2.3

Religion -> *religo*: to tie, to bind
- Respect for what is sacred: awe, piety, rituals, etc.
- We are tied to that which is beyond us humans (God, spirits, universe, etc.)
- What is NOT HUMAN

Religion as an Umbrella Term for Scholarly Study:
- Term used to explain how we organize in communities (relationships) & answer ultimate/universal concerns/questions of all humans (?)
	- Why life/death; good/bad; how we are to be human (what is human); what is beyond us (divinity)?
- acknowledges organized systems that hold people together (Durkheim) → worldviews, traditions, practices, beliefs, ethics, etc.

Working definition: religion is a diverse category of human experiences, organizes people & helps them make sense of reality, which includes being tied to that which is beyond the human (divine, non-human, nature, etc.)

## 2.5

Magic = no universal academic definition
- Historically, a negative contrast to religion & science → "illegitimate" or "primitive" form
- ethnocentric & deviance theory: product of modern Western culture & an "attack" tool in interreligious conflict

Etymology: from *magos* (Greek; pl. *magoi*)
- Priestly caste [status-occupation] in the Persian empire who did rites and ceremonies (mageia)

Working Definition: magic = human control of supernatural forces (an act of human agency in the world)
- lookout for cross-cultural "patterns of magicity" (e.g., ritual use of words → "spells")

Miracle...similarly, there is no one standard way to define
- cross-cultural & cross-religious, however...
- Western/Christian focus & bias: miracles/signs of prophets, esp. Jesus → show either power of God (via intermediary) OR who Christ is (divine)

Etymology: from *miraculum* (Latin): "an object of wonder"
- miror: "I marvel at, admire, am amazed, wonder"

Working Definition: a miracle = event that so defies expectations of the laws of nature that it is presumed that it is supernatural in origin (an act of divine agency made manifest in the world)

*Magos* as a Term of Abuse VS. Historical Reality:
- authentic Magi: hereditary technologists from Iran (Persia)
- charlatans?: Greeks treated Persians as "the Other"

A Limited Scholarly Picture of Historical Magi:
- who: wandering figures (Persian or Hellenized) in the 5th c. BCE onwards
- what: practiced incanctations (charms, songs, whispering) → murmuring = magic
- represent: anxiety over outsiders/foreigners who threatened status quo (e.g., Medea, the non-Greek witch)
- implications: Greeks were dissatisfied with traditional religion (public cult/society) → private/mystery cult (individual)

## 2.7

Project Prompt: Pretend you are an (ancient or modern) follower of the deity, [BLANK]. You have been tasked to create a new ceremony for this divine figure.

1. Introduction & Context:
	- Which **deity** is this?
	- How are they conceived of by their followers? (how they are viewed by community)
	- Why do you follow this deity? (why them, not another)
	- Who are you? (why would you be tasked to do this by the god/your community)
2. The Ceremony: *Magical or Miracle Based*
	- What is this new ceremony?
	- What do you call it?
	- Why do you/your community need this to be created now? (i.e., what is the hoped for result)
	- What makes your ceremony different from all the others that are already performed or produced for this deity?
3. Research & Resources:
	- What sources & resources will you need to craft and perform it?
4. Logistics:
	- Explain the ceremony in detail so that your audience can envision what it would look/feel like if performed. Be detailed!
5. Conclusion & Analysis
	- Explain to me why you chose this deit and why you chose this ceremony's focus
	- How did doing this project help you better undersstand the importance of ritual in religion?
