## 4.23

Paganism: an eclectic grouping of religious traditions that believe in some capacity that Nature is sacred (cf. animism)
- reclamation: Latin paganus/a/um: "related or pertaining to the country-side, rural"; (by extension) "rustic, unlearned" → late medieval: "pagan, heathen"
- focus: pre-Christian traditions as authentic expressions of spirituality → problematic when channeling non-European traditions (appropriation)?
- rise: post-WWII context → turn back to kinship with nature, female principle, desire to do no harm

Neopaganism: a change in meaning/purpose of "paganism"
- old ways: techniques to control natural world → new ways: techniques for individual's transformation
- focus: personal enlightenment
- pros: magic = technology for human growth/potential & involves agency of individual, not grace of divine or result of prayer, etc.
- cons: being utilized in radical hate group's identity formation to claim land/obfuscate the law (cf. Q's Shaman)
