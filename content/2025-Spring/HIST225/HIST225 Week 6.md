## 3.10
### Ancient Judaism (c. 1050 BCE - 70 CE)

**The People of the Levant Region**
- unique in antiquity: text based (Hebrew Bible) & monotheistic ("no idolatry" e.g., golden calf) → contrast: polytheistic neighbors ("the nations")
- narrative cycles of Bible central to theology: sin/exile/enslavement → covenant/return/freedom
- challenge: diaspora (conquered/exiled → spread out); e.g., 586 BCE Babylonian exile & 70 CE destruction of the Temple → wrestled with how to perform Judaism without the Temple (synagogue, cultural aspects, adapt to foreign contexts)

### Ancient Jewish Magic

**Magic in Pre-Modern Judaism**: ambivalent feelings/responses
- condemned: problematic non-Israelite practices in Hebrew Bible (e.g., King Saul seeking out the witch of Edor)
- practiced: often due to diasporic conditions (e.g., incantation bowls)
- emphasis: protection spells and amulets against demons, ghosts, and spirits and mystical connections to the divine

**Prophets of the Lord do Miracles**
- conduits for divine power/will made manifest on earth
- purposes: deploy to create belief in "cultural novelty" of monotheism OR affirm prophet status
- reality: very small category of older historic prophets VS. later prophets who prophesy about future or critique society

**Miracle Types**: defying reality and laws of nature via God
- exorcism (demonic posession) → later, post-biblical demonology = fallen angels/Nephilim
- heal terminally/permanently ill
- raise dead
- defy physics
- note: not necessarily always what modern audiences thing is "nice" or "good" → God-inspired awe isn't always positive!

## 3.12

### Early Christianity

**A Universal Mediterranean Movement?**
- origins: Jesus Movement = Jewish sect (ca. 30 CE) → eventually includes Gentiles
- focus: fulfilling the Law of Moses in the end times under teachings of Jesus
- challenge: apocalypse delayed → institutionalization (the Church)
- doctrine: monotheism with a twist (Trinity = 3 persons, 1 godhead adopted @ Council of Nicaea 325 CE)

**Magic in the New Testament & Ancient Christianity**
- New Testament: magicians = false (Simon Magus)
- reality: magic viewed as real & connected with God's power (Coptic spells)
- terminology changes: God's followers do theurgy ("divine work"), not goeteia ("nonsensical howling")

**Jesus the Magician/Christian Magicians: accusation by 2nd c. CE critics onwards**
- interreligious competition: call your opponents "magicians" = charlatans
- challenges of differentiation: Uncleare whether groups view Jesus's acts as divine agency (divine Jesus) or as human agency (Jesus using supernatural powers like other magic workers)
- Jesus multiplying th eloaves with a rhabdos

## 3.14

Jesus, Prophet of the Lord: a biblical presumption with a twist
- same: repeat miracles of HB prophets (Elijah) = God's power (monotheism)
- different: messiah ("annointed" like a king, not oil but water upon his baptism) → fulfill prophesy: "son of David" will free from oppression

What Kind of Jesus?: changes in views about JC over time in Gospels
- Synoptics (Mark, Matt, Luke): human prophet → miracles = God's will (mercy)
- Johannine (John): divine Word → signs = prove he IS God (identity)
- compare: miracles (dunameis) = indirect divine agencry in synoptics VS. signs (semeia) = direct divine agency in John
- so what: later doctrine of the Trinity (Jesus equal to Father a& Holy Spirit, equally human & divine) is not found in gospels (John is source)
- example: Christ Pantocrator (Christ is the All Powerful)

Holy Text = Power:
- if religious authority is dependent on written word, then its use/abuse is tied with power
- restricted access: prior to the printing press, few copies & held mostly by elite
- anxiety vs. innovation: holy texts may be misused/mistranslated OR using the texts in new, creative ways

Writings within Objects: fragments of protective sacred text
- objects: talisman (Greek telesma "payment") & amulets
- belief even small portions of sacred text have inherent power
- example: Tefillin (Tora in leather boxes)

Grimoires: name for magical spell-books (esp. 18th c. France)
- French → Greek *gramma* "that which is drawn or written"
- instruction manuals: encyclopedias containing all kinds of spells, especially those cencerning alchemy & summoning demons
- purpose: create permenant, physical record of magic knowledge in contrast to oral traditions

Technological Innovations in Writing:
- papyris → parchment → printing press (1436)
- religous/magical texts = more accessible (democratic), at the expense of power?
