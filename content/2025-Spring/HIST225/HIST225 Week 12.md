## 4.30

Canonization: the final processs by which a deceased person is placed in the catalogue of saints in the Roman Catholic Church
- what & why: perpetual worship (veneration and invocation) because they proved they were closer to the divine = cloud of witnesses
- origins: local saints worshipped in (home)towns, overseen by bishops (often martyrs) → 10th century papal organization via bishop collection of testimony & decree for diocese → 12th century Holy See (vatican)

Medical Miracles in the Roman Catholic Church
- Vatican's recognition of saints (canonization): based on evidence (proof) that they worked miracles → signs of their proximity to God
- 16th to 20th c. CE: most miracles are healings, involving medical care & substantial physician testimony, and adapt to increasing medicalization of health (more doctors, new tech/diagnoses)
- "dramatic arc" in narratives: patients consistently follow a standard structure of suffering, invocation (via prayer/pilgrimage, relics), healing (with dreams/visions), and thanksgiving (prayers, offerings, etc)

Physician's Role in Vatican Investigations: 2 important steps
1. diagnose condition as hopeless
2. express surprise at the outcome
- note: all therapies must be attempted and natural causes eliminated before Vatican will declare a miracle
