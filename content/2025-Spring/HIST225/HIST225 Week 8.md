## 4.2

European Intellectual Transition: the Renaissance and Enlightenment

Onion Magic: The Onions of Somerset: Sympathetic magic?
- Sympathetic magic: do to an object, happen to person (voodoo dolls)
- Local tradition: pins in object identified with people

The Renaissance: "rebirth period" 14th-17th c.
- Focus: intentional "rediscovery" of classical tradition

The Enlightenment: intellectual movement 17th-18th c.
- Focus: individualism/science over tradition/religion
- Philosophers movement: Descartes, Locke, Newton

Period of Transition in Eope: medieval → early modernity
- Positivist view on humanity: humans can learn / reason against ignorance of past humans
- Enlightenment killed belief in low class "old magic" in favor of elite "scientific magic"
	- Acceptable: learned magic tied to science (e.g. alchemy, neoplatonism)
	- Unacceptable: Old "superstitious" magic (witchcraft, folklore)

John Dee (1500): Elizabethan "conjurer" or early scientist?
- context: age of religious tumult (negativity) → protestant reformation
- specialties: magical advice to the court ... math, map-making, astronomy

Isaac Newton (1600): Founding father of science or the "last magician"?
- context: age of humanism (positivity) → understand universe = God
- specialties: physics (3 laws, prisms, gravity) ... alchemy, astrology, biblical prophesy

So What?: implicit bias in history-telling of the modern "rational" world
- presumed dualism: contrast good (science, humanism) vs bad (magic, religion) → reality = complicated overlap

The Protestant Reformation: Internal Critique of Catholic Church (unity) → Schism into denominations
- 1517 Martin Luther
- Crticism: abuses of practices in the church as an institution (wealth, crimes, indulgences → purgatory)
- protestant theoloty: personal faith, not deeds lead to salvation

Protestant Rhetoric: Catholic practicies (sacraments) are ineffectual "magic"
- superstitous OR inneffectual
- Presumptions: humans cannot access divine power
- implications: only faith saves/heals if God chooses (od Christian magic were fake)

Case Study: Reginald Scot's discovery of witchcraft 1584
- Exposé treates on witchcraft: in reality, a protestant attack on catholocism
- Aerasatiens of witchcraft are irrational → result of catholic church
- presumption: magic is mostly an illusion
- implications: first technical guides on "how to do witchcraft"
- ironically became a guide on how to do magic

demon: an evil supernatural spirit
- daimon (Greek): "(lower) god; guardian spirit/patron" → "divides & allots supplies"
- shift from positive to negative perspective in late antiquity → demons = negative forces permitted by God as tests of faith (monotheism)
- examples: Roman Lar (household) & Penates (pantry) as protectors (top: 1st  CE house-shrine, Pompeii) VS. Life of St Antony's fight with hostile demons in desert
- demonology: the "science" of demons & belief in them → part of larger scholarly efforts to understand reality/God (height: 15th c. to 18th c. CE Europe)

Devil (Middle English): the chief devil; Statan
- Diabolos: "false accuser, slanderer" → "Tempter" (NT)

The Demonic Role Changes: From God's Agents to Rebellious Figures
- 15th c. CE onwards: witches viewed as part of organized Saanic sect & demons act independently from God (no longer viewed as acting by God's permission as tests of faith)
- why (challenge): if God is Goodness (cf. Augustine), is it logical that Goodness would permit such evil? That implies that God is the source of sin.
- representation: demonic forces now began to represent perversion of the gift of free will as rebellion/resistance → also an inherited human tendency to do evil because of Adam & Eve (doctrine of original sin)
- how: developments in Devil's backstory over time (spoiler: God's "right hand man" becomes chief problem), which can then be tied to humans as the creators of their own sin & suffering
